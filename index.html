Overview

Before AI can market a business, it has to understand the story the owner is already living.

Project Bridgeway is Adobe's AI marketing agent for small businesses. Before it understands the business, it asks the owner to choose channels, configure tools, and translate their needs into marketing terminology. I redesigned onboarding as a translation layer: five questions in the owner's own words, then useful work prepared for their review.

Stage 01. The friction

0+

steps in the current onboarding.

Desktop-only workflows, extensive configuration, and tedious manual setups that block immediate value creation for busy small business owners.

Friction 01

Requires manual prompt copying to external AI toolsThe product hands the work of translation back to the owner.

Friction 02

Desktop-first layout isolates mobile business usersOwners run their businesses away from a desk. The flow assumes they are sitting at one.

Two languages

The product and the owner are speaking different languages.

Who needs me today?

What is your campaign objective?

What should people remember?

Which task should an agent run?

What will happen without me?

What audience should we target?

The friction is not only in the interface. It is in the product's mental model.

I expected to find a marketing workflow problem. Instead, I found a translation problem.

Small-business owners already understand what makes their business meaningful. The product's job is to translate that understanding into useful work.

The translation model

Do not ask owners to plan marketing. Learn the story they already tell every day.

01

Owner context

Words, website, Instagram, reviews, schedule, and customer history.

02

Business essence

Bridgeway identifies the values, audience truths, and language that make the business distinct.

03

Marketing direction

It translates that understanding into a focused strategy.

04

Useful work

It prepares relevant drafts in the places where the business already operates.

05

Owner approval

Nothing is published or sent until the owner reviews it.

Stage 02. The strategy

Our approach

An intentional shift toward simplicity, focusing strictly on high-impact insights.

01

People use their phone first

Onboarding happens on the go. Small business owners manage their physical spaces, clients, and assets away from their desks. Mobile-first design guarantees onboarding is accessible instantly, anywhere.

02

Onboarding needs to be short

Cut the noise. Minimize the steps down to a single narrative flow: establish business name, select tone alignment, identify customer takeaway thoughts, link core channels, and provide direct validation.

Principles of intentional UI

Language

Meet owners in their languageTranslate professional marketing actions into native human objectives. Jargon is cognitive overhead.

Sequence

Reflect before recommendingShow owners what you understand about them before prompting for more decisions. Ground the relationship.

Attention

Work quietly and ask deliberatelyAutomate downstream structural choices. Reserve human attention for curation and intent.

Value

Start with owner words and recognizable situationsDeliver instantly tangible assets. Let value precede setup configuration.

Control

Show what Bridgeway understood before it recommends workAI is the engine, but the business owner always holds the steering wheel. Build absolute confidence.

Evidence discipline

Observed

Onboarding begins with marketing interests and configuration before establishing business context.

Inferred

Owners must translate their business needs into the product's marketing language.

Hypothesis

Beginning with owner language will improve trust and reduce time to first useful work.

Not validated

The quality of the reflection, the minimum context required, and whether the model works consistently across business types.

What I would validate next

Recognition

Can owners recognize themselves in the opening choices without additional explanation?

Trust

Does the reflection feel accurate enough for owners to connect business sources?

Control

Can owners clearly explain what Bridgeway will prepare and what it will never send without approval?

Measures

Time to first useful draft. Percentage of drafts approved, edited, held, or rejected. Owner comprehension of automation boundaries. Confidence before and after the reflection. Performance across different business categories.
